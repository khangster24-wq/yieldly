# Yieldly — 2-Minute Demo Script

A walkthrough for showing Yieldly to a teacher, judge, or college application reader. Goal: land the finance-portfolio concept, not just click through screens. Practice it until it's ~90 seconds to 2 minutes without rushing.

## The hook (15 sec)

> "Every college search tool treats admissions and money as separate problems. I treat them as one — the same way an investor thinks about a stock. Every school has a **risk** — your admission odds — and a **return** — what it actually costs you versus what it pays off. Yieldly is a college list built like an investment portfolio."

## 1. Swipe Discovery (20 sec)

Open **Discover**. Swipe through 2-3 real schools.

> "Every card is real data — U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard numbers, not guesses. Net price after aid, risk tier, and a 1-5 ROI score, right up front. Swipe right to add it to my portfolio."

Point out one international school if one appears (e.g. a UK or Australian school) — "and it's not just U.S. schools; I added real cost and admissions data for the schools international/IB students target too."

## 2. Portfolio Dashboard — the concept payoff (35 sec)

Switch to **Portfolio** (ideally with 5-8 schools already saved).

> "This is the concept made visible. This donut is my diversification — reach, target, safety — exactly like an asset allocation chart. Right now I'm [reach-heavy / balanced], and it tells me so, the same way a robo-advisor flags an unbalanced portfolio."

Tap a school row open.

> "And every score is explainable — I show my work. This isn't a black-box machine learning model claiming to predict your future; it's a transparent formula anyone can check: admit rate adjusted for your GPA and SAT, ROI from real net cost versus real earnings data. That honesty is deliberate — a college chancing tool that can't explain itself isn't trustworthy."

## 3. Scholarship Yield Finder (20 sec)

Switch to **Scholarships**.

> "Same investing lens: everyone ranks scholarships by dollar amount. I rank by **expected value** — win probability times payout. A $50,000 no-essay lottery scholarship is actually a worse bet than a $2,500 award you're likely to win. That's a real finance concept — expected value — applied somewhere nobody else applies it."

## 4. AI Counselor (20 sec)

Switch to **Advisor**, ask a real question ("Which scholarships should I prioritize?").

> "The counselor isn't generic — it's reading my actual saved portfolio and scholarship matches live, so every number it says back to me is real, not hallucinated. It leads with financial strategy first, because that's the gap I noticed: every other college AI is generic; none of them are money-first."

## Close (10 sec)

> "The whole app is one idea followed all the way through: a college list is a portfolio, and thinking about it that way — risk, return, diversification, yield — makes you a smarter applicant. That's the thesis, and it's why I built it this way instead of just another swipe app."

## If asked "why does this exist / what's the gap?"

Point to `docs/PRD.md`'s problem framing: existing tools each own one lane (CollegeVine = chancing + pricey advising, Niche = reviews with a trust problem, indie swipe apps = no real rigor). None treat cost/ROI as a first-class citizen next to admission odds — that's the gap, and it's the one a finance-minded student is positioned to notice.

## If asked "is any of this fake data?"

No — say so directly: U.S. schools are real College Scorecard data; international schools are researched from university-published fees and government sources (UK LEO, Australia's QILT), clearly labeled as such in the app; scholarships are real, curated, current programs; the risk/ROI scores are Yieldly's own transparent formulas, always labeled as estimates, never claimed as guarantees or ML predictions.
